Clinical History

39-year-old female with 6-week history of left knee pain following recreational running. Patient reports pain is worse with weight-bearing activities, climbing stairs, and prolonged sitting. Some swelling noted. No history of acute trauma or previous knee surgery. Physical exam reveals mild effusion and medial joint line tenderness. Referring physician requesting MRI to evaluate for meniscal tear and assess cartilage integrity.

Technique

MRI of the left knee was performed using a 1.5 Tesla MRI scanner with dedicated knee coil. Multiplanar imaging was obtained including sagittal T1-weighted, T2-weighted, proton density, and coronal T2-weighted sequences. Axial T2-weighted images were also acquired. No intravenous contrast was administered per protocol.

Findings
Menisci: The lateral meniscus appears intact with normal morphology and signal intensity. There is a small horizontal tear involving the posterior horn of the medial meniscus (grade 3 signal extending to the articular surface). No displaced fragments are identified.
Cruciate Ligaments: The anterior cruciate ligament (ACL) demonstrates normal morphology and signal characteristics. The posterior cruciate ligament (PCL) is intact.
Collateral Ligaments: The medial collateral ligament (MCL) and lateral collateral ligament (LCL) appear intact with normal signal intensity.
Articular Cartilage: Mild thinning of the articular cartilage is noted along the medial femoral condyle consistent with early degenerative changes (grade 2 chondromalacia). The lateral compartment cartilage appears preserved.
Bone Marrow: No evidence of bone marrow edema, fracture, or focal osseous lesions. Normal bone marrow signal throughout the distal femur, proximal tibia, and patella.
Joint Effusion: Small to moderate joint effusion is present within the suprapatellar recess and posterior joint space.
Soft Tissues: No significant soft tissue abnormalities. The patellar tendon and quadriceps tendon appear intact. Baker's cyst is not present.
Impression
  1. Small horizontal tear of the posterior horn of the medial meniscus. This finding is consistent with the patient's clinical symptoms and physical examination.
  2. Mild degenerative changes of the medial compartment with grade 2 chondromalacia of the medial femoral condyle.
  3. Small to moderate knee joint effusion.
  4. Intact cruciate and collateral ligaments. No evidence of ligamentous injury.
  5. No acute fracture or bone marrow abnormalities.
Recommendations
  • Orthopedic consultation for evaluation of meniscal tear treatment options
  • Consider conservative management initially with physical therapy and activity modification
  • Anti-inflammatory medications may help reduce effusion and symptoms
  • Arthroscopic evaluation may be considered if conservative management fails
  • Weight-bearing restrictions and knee bracing as clinically indicated
