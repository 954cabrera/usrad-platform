Clinical History

39-year-old female presenting with persistent headaches, dizziness, and occasional visual disturbances for the past 3 months. Patient reports headaches are more frequent in the morning and worsen with physical activity. No history of head trauma. Referring physician requesting evaluation to rule out structural abnormalities.

Technique

MRI of the brain was performed using a 3.0 Tesla MRI scanner. Multiplanar T1-weighted, T2-weighted, FLAIR, and diffusion-weighted imaging sequences were obtained. Gadolinium-based contrast agent (Gadavist 0.1 mmol/kg) was administered intravenously for post-contrast T1-weighted imaging in axial, sagittal, and coronal planes.

Findings
Brain Parenchyma: The cerebral hemispheres demonstrate normal signal intensity on all pulse sequences. No evidence of acute infarction, hemorrhage, or mass lesion. The gray-white matter differentiation is preserved bilaterally.
Ventricular System: The lateral, third, and fourth ventricles are normal in size and configuration. No evidence of hydrocephalus or midline shift.
Extra-axial Spaces: The subarachnoid spaces are unremarkable. No evidence of subdural or epidural collections.
Posterior Fossa: The brainstem and cerebellum appear normal. The cerebellopontine angles are clear bilaterally.
Vascular Structures: The major intracranial vessels demonstrate normal flow voids. No evidence of aneurysm or vascular malformation on this non-angiographic study.
Sinuses and Mastoids: Mild mucosal thickening in the bilateral maxillary sinuses, likely representing acute sinusitis. The frontal, ethmoid, and sphenoid sinuses are clear. Mastoid air cells are well-aerated bilaterally.
Post-Contrast Enhancement: No abnormal parenchymal enhancement. Normal enhancement of the choroid plexus and venous structures.
Impression
  1. No acute intracranial abnormality. Specifically, no evidence of mass lesion, acute infarction, or hemorrhage.
  2. Mild bilateral maxillary sinusitis - clinical correlation recommended. This finding may explain the patient's headache symptoms.
  3. Normal brain MRI with contrast - The persistent headaches are likely not related to structural brain abnormalities.
Recommendations
  • Clinical correlation with ENT evaluation for sinusitis management
  • Consider tension-type headache or migraine evaluation if symptoms persist
  • Follow-up imaging not indicated unless clinical symptoms change significantly
  • Patient education regarding normal findings and symptomatic treatment options
