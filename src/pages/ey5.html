How a Workers' Compensation crisis in 1994 led to a healthcare model that saved $585 million and is now evolving to serve 90 million Americans.

It Started With a Simple Question

"Why does an MRI cost $3,000 at a hospital but $600 at an outpatient center two blocks away?"

In 1994, while managing Workers' Compensation claims, we discovered that injured workers waited an average of 14 days for imaging that determined their entire treatment path. The human cost was immeasurable. The financial cost was destroying businesses.

We saw a broken system: Hospitals had monopolized imaging, driving up prices. Insurance companies had no leverage. Patients had no choices. Quality outpatient centers sat empty while hospitals had two-week backlogs.

The Breakthrough:

What if we could create a network where imaging centers competed for patients based on quality, speed, and price? What if transparency could fix healthcare?

That question led to AnciCare. America's first managed care imaging network.
